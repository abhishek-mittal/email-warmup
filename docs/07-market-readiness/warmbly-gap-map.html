Where EmailWarm stands against Warmbly

Warmbly is an open-source cold outreach and warmup platform. I read its code. On warmup alone we are a credible, well-tested early version. As a platform, we are far behind. Closing that gap means choosing a narrower product, not copying theirs.

The short answer

Where we are level

Core warmup mechanics

Ramped daily volume, jittered spaced sends, hard caps, bounce-rate pause, authentication checks and GDPR-style erase and export all exist on both sides.

Where we are behind on warmup

Trust and realism

No verified-delivery tokens, no tamper detection, no graded health bands, no reciprocity weighting, and no real network of mailboxes to warm against.

Where we are far behind

Enterprise platform

No organizations or roles, SSO, 2FA, audit log, public API, webhooks, admin console, worker fleet or observability. Warmbly has all of these.

What to learn from their warmup engine

These are design ideas that are cheap to port and raise trust. They are in Go, so we reimplement them in TypeScript rather than copy files.

1. Verified-delivery tokens

Every warmup email carries a platform-minted token, single use and bound to its recipient. It stops replay and proves a mailbox really received the mail.

impact higheffort smalltheir warmup_token flow

2. Tamper attribution

If a mailbox deletes or spam-flags warmup mail it verifiably received, only its owner could have done it. They count that as a strike on a ladder, not a first-offense ban.

impact higheffort mediumwarmup_tampering

3. Graded health bands

Healthy, watch, throttled, quarantined, blocked. Throttled means half volume and double spacing while warmup keeps running. Spam placement only slows a mailbox, never bans it.

impact higheffort mediumevaluateMetrics

4. Placement read over verified deliveries

Rates come from verified receipts at Google, Microsoft and Yahoo only, with a floor of 20 deliveries in 7 days. Small hosts never build a headline number.

impact higheffort smallwarmup_placement_sql

5. Reciprocity and partner diversity

Pick partners with weights for recent use, domain frequency and how far behind each mailbox is on what it sent. Cross-tier fallback when the pool is small.

impact mediumeffort mediumWarmupPartnerCandidates

6. Warmup to cold graduation

A mailbox leaves warmup at 5, 10 or 20 cold sends a day depending on how long it warmed, then adds 5 per clean day.

impact mediumeffort smallwarmupramp/cold.go

7. Control plane and worker fleet

Workers never touch the database and send through each mailbox's own provider, so throughput scales by adding workers. This is their main architectural bet.

impact higheffort largeKafka or event bus

8. Operator tooling

An admin app, a CLI that works when sign-in does, an instance health page and a doctor command. They treat operations as a product surface.

impact mediumeffort mediumwarmblyctl, admin/

An honest read of both sides

PointDetail
Our small edgesA send ledger with an explicit uncertain state, so a lost acknowledgement is never blindly resent. Placement results that exclude unreachable seeds instead of counting them as spam. Scores that say how much is measured. Egress limits on customer mail hosts. I did not confirm the absence of these in Warmbly, so treat them as areas where we are at least deliberate, not proven ahead.
Their real moatA network of monitored mailboxes to warm against, plus years of breadth. Warmup quality depends on pool size and trust. We have no customers, so our shared pool is empty and our placement tests have no seed mailboxes.
Their own gapsTheir internal deliverability report lists open-loop ramps, shallow threads, and worker bounce results not feeding the health model. Part of that report is stale: the auth sweep and cold graduation it calls missing now exist in code. Docs lag the code there, so do not rely on it.
Scope warning"Enterprise Warmbly" is campaigns, CRM, inbox, automations, forms, tracking and an iOS app on top of warmup. Rebuilding that is years of work. A warmup and deliverability product with enterprise controls is reachable.

Path to an enterprise warmup product

Order matters: a warmup network nobody trusts cannot be rescued by SSO. Each phase has an exit test, not a date.

Phase A

A pool worth joining

  • Verified-delivery tokens, tamper strikes, graded health bands
  • Placement over verified receipts at the big three, with a sample floor
  • Provision seed mailboxes and a Spamhaus key; run a real two-domain warm cycle
  • Reciprocity-weighted partner choice; graduation ramp

Exit: 14 days of real warming across at least 3 providers with measured inbox rate and no manual intervention.

Phase B

Enterprise controls

  • Organizations, members, roles and permission checks on every route
  • OIDC single sign-on, then SAML and SCIM; 2FA
  • Audit log; API keys with scopes; signed outbound webhooks
  • Key rotation and optional KMS for stored secrets

Exit: a tenant-isolation test suite that fails if any route returns another organization's data.

Phase C

Run it like a service

  • Split API from workers; worker heartbeat in readiness
  • Metrics and tracing; central logs; runbooks; restore rehearsal
  • Admin console and operator CLI for stops, seeds, holds
  • Rate limits; browser tests; deploy pipeline run end to end

Exit: kill a worker mid-send and restore from backup, both rehearsed, with measured recovery time.

Phase D

Scale and sell

  • Dedicated pools per enterprise customer; data residency choice
  • Self-host installer for customers who want it
  • Real billing; provider app approval; privacy and terms pages

Exit: a paying pilot customer on a written service level.
